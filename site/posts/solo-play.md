Historically they didn't work adventure/dungeon generation systems by themselves are aids but not an engine.  Skip forward to now and there are a few things we need to understand. I use a deeper series of random inspiration than just prompts.  So lets start with the first one and some key definitions I built.

1d10 Oracle +Luck+3
2 or less: Unlucky? No and a complication
3-4 : No and it takes a turn for the worse
5. : No but there is a sign of trouble or reveal a clue
6. : No but there is a glimmer of benefit or reveal a clue.
7. : Maybe, what are you willing to risk or take a No? 
8. : Yes but at some cost or take a No. 50% of finding a clue in addition if a No is taken.
9-10. : Yes
11-12. : Yes and it is better than expected. 50% of finding a clue in addition.
13-14. : Lucky!
15.: Here and higher the character is so lucky it starts rubbing off. Next oracle roll by another character is at +1 in this character's favor. 

Note this is specifically built for the Chrome system (Luck might be vastly different in other games for example)

Glossary of Oracular Terms



Lucky: title, no mechanical meaning. Unlucky: title, no mechanical meaning.

Clue: This should be something to help the character resolve an in game puzzle/issue.

Yes: Whatever the question is, the answer to the base Yes/No question is an affirmative to the question. This can be good or bad depending on context.

No: Whatever the question is, the answer to the base Yes/No question is a negative to the question. This can be good or bad depending on context.

Maybe: Usually this adds a condition to the answer allowing a success or failure to be pursued or walked away from at the choice of the person consulting the Oracle.

Complicated: Most questions are asked and answered with simple clear answers.  This time the answer is supposed to muddy the waters. (un)fortunately your subconscious is working on this for you. 

Complication: Something goes wrong, just enough to where until the character has a chance to fix the issue they suffer about as much difficulty as a cyberware item failure, typically related to the ability that would be used to solve the issue.

Better: The result is a marked improvement over the base pitch of the question. 

Worse: The result is a marked downgrade over the base pitch of the question.

Looks: The aim for this modifier is to allow a short twist within the question pitched, adding some narrative length for an emotional range.

Disaster: This is a ‘situation goes wrong’ which is different than a Yes to a negative outcome or a No to a positive outcome. Things should be twice as difficult as a complication, requiring probably 50% more positive results than before to correct.
